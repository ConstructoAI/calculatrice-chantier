1. Overview

The Constructo AI calculator works the way people talk on a jobsite: in feet, inches and fractions, as well as in metres and millimetres. It brings three things together.

  • The framer's keypad: pitches and rafters, hips, valleys and jack rafters, stairs, rake walls and arched walls, arcs and circles, compound mitres, material quantities, conversions and costs.
  • The keypad of your trade: 76 tools for 11 other trades. Tap your trade above the keypad, and the top keys become those of its tools.
  • The calculation tape, which keeps a record of everything that was entered and calculated, so you can review it, save it or share it.

The calculator is free, with no ads and no account. Nothing is sent over the Internet: your settings, memories and tapes stay on your device.

Disclaimer. This calculator is a calculation aid. It does not replace the drawings, the judgment of a qualified person, or the requirements of the building code in force where you work (in Québec, the Construction Code) and of municipal regulations. Always check your results before cutting or ordering.

The trade tools do geometry, physics and arithmetic. They do not reproduce any code table (ampacities, minimum wire sizes, minimum slopes, permitted fill) and contain no prices: you enter your own costs and rates.

2. Installing and opening Contents

On the Web: computer, tablet, phone

Open constructoai.github.io/calculatrice-chantier. The calculator runs as is, in the browser.

Installing it on a phone or tablet

Once installed, the calculator has its own icon, opens full screen and works offline, even at the far end of a basement.

  • Android (Chrome): open the site, then the calculator's ☰ menu, and tap Install the app. Or, in Chrome's menu: Install app.
  • iPhone and iPad (Safari): open the site, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Computer (Chrome or Edge): the calculator's ☰ menu, Install the app, or the install icon in the address bar.

Updates arrive on their own: each time it opens with a network connection, the calculator picks up the latest version. The current version is written at the bottom of the ☰ menu.

On Windows, without a browser

Download calculatrice-chantier-windows-x64.exe from the latest release, then run it: there is nothing to install. The executable is not signed; the first time you run it, Windows may display "Windows protected your PC": choose More info, then Run anyway.

4. Entering a measurement and calculating Contents

Measurements

Enter a measurement the way you say it: the number, then the unit.

If you do not type the denominator of a fraction, the current resolution is used: 15 / means 15/16. The ⌫ key erases the last character typed.

Operations

The + − × ÷ and = keys work as on any calculator, with measurements as well as with numbers. Calculations chain from left to right.

Example 5 Feet 7 Inch 1 / 2 + 18 Feet 8 Inch = → 24 ft 3 1/2 in

A length times a length gives an area; an area times a length gives a volume.

Example 40 Feet × 35 Feet = → 1,400 ft²

Percentages

+ 10 % adds 10% to the value — your waste, in one move. − 20 % takes 20% off, × 18 % calculates 18% of it, ÷ 80 % divides it by 0.80.

Conversions

With a value displayed, press the unit you want: m converts it to metres, Feet to feet, Conv 9 to millimetres. Feet toggles between feet-inches and decimal feet, Inch between fraction and decimals.

The Conv key

Conv is the conversion and second-function key. Followed by a key, it runs the function written in small type above that key; followed by a unit, it converts the displayed value. You can also tap the small label directly.

Clearing

  • Clear, once: clears the display and any error.
  • Clear, twice: also clears the temporary values (rise, run, length, diameter…). Do this before a new problem. The entered pitch, the memories and the stored values are kept.
  • Conv × (Clear all): restores the calculator to new, after confirmation — memories, tape, stored values and the fields of the trade tool. Preferences are kept.

Copying and pasting

Double-clicking the displayed value copies it (or Ctrl+C). Ctrl+V pastes a number or a length written out: 12 ft 6 in, 30 in, 3.8 m.

5. Memories Contents

  • The cumulative memory. Each press of M+ adds the displayed value to the total; Conv M+ subtracts it. Recall M+ shows the total; M+ after that shows the average, then the number of values. Recall Recall shows the total and clears the memory.
  • Three free registers. Store 1, 2 or 3 stores the displayed value; Recall 1, 2 or 3 brings it back.
  • The calculator's stored values. Store 4 to 9 and 0 set the block, the on-centre spacing, the footing section, the target riser, the floor thickness, the target tread and the density. They can also be changed in preferences.
  • Recalling what a key holds in memory. Recall followed by a function key shows its last value: Recall Run shows the last run.

6. The framing keypad Contents

This is the basic keypad, the one the calculator shows when the trade bar reads Framing. Its function keys all follow the same rule: a value typed, then the key, stores the value; the key alone calculates. Press a key again to cycle through all its results.

Example A rafter: 7 Inch Pitch · 8 Feet 5 Inch Run · Diag → 9 ft 8 15/16 in, then Hip Valley → 12 ft 10 1/2 in

The top keys solve the right triangle: enter two values among Pitch, Rise, Run and Diag, then press the third. The pitch stays in memory: no need to enter it again for the next rafter. To send a result to another function, press = first.

Each key and each second function has its own topic, with a worked example, in the directory of keys and functions below.

7. The keypad of your trade Contents

Above the keypad, the trade bar starts with a solid chip: the current trade (Framing, at first). To its right, a row of chips scrolls sideways.

  • Choosing your trade: tap it in the row, or tap the solid chip to open the Your trade list.
  • Changing tools: once the trade is chosen, the row shows its tools. One tap is all it takes.
  • Going back to framing: tap the solid chip, then Framing, roofing and stairs.

The calculator remembers the current tool: you find it again the next time you open it.

Filling in a tool

The tool's fields become the top keys. Above each key is written the content of its field; when the field is empty, you read there, in parentheses, the unit of a number typed without a unit: (ft), (in), (A).

  1. Type a value, then press the key of the field where you want to store it. A bare number takes the field's unit: 24 on a field in feet gives 24 ft 0 in.
  2. A dark key cycles through the options of a list: the angle of a fitting, the size of a conductor.
  3. Press Result. The calculation is recorded on the tape and the first result is displayed, with its rank: 2 of 3. Press again to see the next one.

Example A slab 24 ft by 20 ft, 4 in thick. Concrete trade, Concrete slab tool: 24 Feet Length · 20 Feet Width · 4 Inch Thickness · Result → 480 ft² (44.6 m²) · Result → 5.93 yd³ (4.53 m³) · Result → 6.52 yd³ (4.98 m³) to order, 10% waste included.

Example A 12 in offset with 45° fittings. Plumbing trade, Pipe offset tool: 12 Inch Offset · Result → 1 ft 5 in of travel · Result → 1 ft 0 in of run. For another angle, press the dark Angle key until you reach the angle you want.

Example The voltage drop of a 120 V, 15 A circuit, 100 ft long, in 14 AWG copper. Electrical trade, Voltage drop tool: 15 Current · 100 Feet Length · Result → 9.42 V · Result → 7.85% · Result → 110.6 V at the load.

Good to know

  • Recalling a field: its key, with nothing typed, displays its value.
  • Resetting a field to its starting value: Conv, then its key. All the fields: tap the Start over label above Result.
  • A value is missing? The display names it: MISSING: WIDTH.
  • Keep calculating: a displayed result is a value like any other. × 2 =, M+ and Store all use it.
  • From one tool to the next: the displayed value follows you when you change tools. The loose volume of a trench, for example, goes into the Volume field of the truckloads tool with one press.
  • An area calculated on the keypad goes into a field in square feet: 40 Feet × 35 Feet =, then Area.
  • How to use a tool: press and hold one of its keys.

The same tools as forms

The Trades tab presents each tool as a form: all the fields and all the results at a glance, recalculated with every keystroke. A measurement is written out in full there (12 ft 6 in, 30 in, 3.8 m). The Add to tape button records the calculation; On keypad plugs the tool into the keys of the home screen. The form and the keypad share the same values.

Results follow the system of the first length you entered: in feet-inches or in metric for lengths, in both systems for areas, volumes and weights. Quantities to buy (bags, boxes, sheets) are rounded up to the next whole unit.

All the tools are described, with an example, in the directory of tools by trade.

8. The calculation tape Contents

The tape keeps a record of your calculations: the operations and their totals, the values given to functions and their results, the conversions, and the details of each trade tool calculation, under its title.

  • Viewing it: the Tapes tab, or Conv =. In a wide window, it stays on screen to the right of the calculator.
  • Saving: give it a name — the client's or the jobsite's. Saved tapes can be reopened, renamed, copied, exported and deleted from the Tapes tab.
  • Copying: the text of the tape goes to the clipboard, ready to paste into an email or a text message.
  • Exporting: a .txt file.
  • Clearing: erases the current tape, after confirmation.

The current tape keeps the last 500 lines. Clear twice draws a separator line on it; Clear all empties it.

The language

The FR | EN button in the top bar switches the whole calculator from French to English and back: the keys, the display, the tools, the help and this manual. The way numbers are written and the unit symbols follow the language: 1 234,5 pi² in French, 1,234.5 ft² in English. Your values do not change: what you typed in a tool is rewritten in the new language. The first time it opens, the calculator takes the language of the device if it is French or English; after that, it keeps the one you chose.

In English, the decimal separator is always the point, and a comma typed between groups of three digits separates thousands: 1,500 is one thousand five hundred. In French, the comma is always the decimal separator. Units, however, can be typed in either language: 12 pi 6 po as well as 12 ft 6 in.

11. Pro tips Contents

  1. Cycle through the results. Press a function key again to see all its results: Diag gives the length, then the plumb cut, then the level cut.
  2. The pitch stays in memory. No need to enter it again for the next rafter.
  3. Start fresh. Before a new problem, press Clear twice.
  4. Reuse a result. To send a result to another function, press = first.
  5. Check for square. Length, Width, then Width twice: the diagonal to measure on site.
  6. Add your waste in one move. + 10 %.
  7. Convert effortlessly. With a value displayed, press the unit you want. Feet toggles between feet-inches and decimal feet.
  8. Click the label. Clicking the small label above a key is the same as Conv then the key.
  9. Keep your cut list. Save the tape after calculating a stair or a roof.
  10. Need more precision? Set the fraction resolution in Preferences, or temporarily: Conv 3 for 1/32.
  11. Round up. Material quantities are exact, not rounded: always order the next whole unit.
  12. On a keyboard. The apostrophe for feet, the quotation mark for inches, the space bar for Conv.
  13. Your trade at your fingertips. Tap the trade name above the keypad so the top keys become those of your tools.
  14. From one tool to the next. A displayed result follows you when you change tools: press the key of the field where you want to store it.
  15. In English or in French. The FR | EN button at the top of the screen switches the language instantly: numbers and units follow (1 234,5 pi² becomes 1,234.5 ft²), and your values stay the same.

12. Messages and troubleshooting Contents

The display showsWhat it means
NO VALUEThe key has nothing to recall or calculate: it is missing a value. Type it, then press again.
MISSING: …The trade tool is still waiting for the value of the field named.

ERROR – cannot calculateThe calculation has no result: a division by zero, an impossible triangle. Press Clear.
ERROR – trigonometryThe value is outside the function's domain: an inverse sine greater than 1, for example. Press Clear.
ERROR – overflowThe result is too large for the display. Press Clear.
  • A result looks rounded. Lengths are displayed at the fraction resolution chosen in preferences (to 1/16 of an inch, at first); the calculation itself keeps its full precision.
  • A framing function gives an unexpected result. A value from an earlier calculation may still be in memory: press Clear twice and start again.
  • The framing keys have disappeared. A trade tool is on the keypad: tap the trade chip, then Framing, roofing and stairs.
  • The calculator is in the wrong language. Tap FR or EN in the top bar.
  • The installed calculator does not have the latest version. Open it once with a network connection, close it, then open it again. The version is shown at the bottom of the ☰ menu.
  • My tapes have disappeared (Web version). They are kept in the browser: they are erased along with the site's browsing data, and do not follow you from one device to another. Export the ones that matter.

13. Directory of keys and functions Contents

These are the topics of the Help tab: one for each key and each second function of the framing keypad, with a worked example.

The main keys

Pitch

The pitch of a roof or of an inclined plane. It is expressed in inches of rise per 12 inches of run (7 in = a 7/12 pitch), in degrees, as a percentage or as a ratio. To enter it: 7 Inch Pitch (7/12), 30 Pitch (30 degrees) or 75 % Pitch (75%). To calculate it: enter two values among the rise, the run and the diagonal, then press Pitch. Press again to see it in its other forms. The pitch you enter stays in memory until you enter another one.

Example 30.45 Pitch, then Pitch repeatedly → 30.45° · 58.78702% · ratio 0.58787 · 7 1/16 in

Rise

The vertical side of a right triangle. It is the height of a roof from the top plate to the ridge, the total height of a stair from one finished floor to the next, or the rise of an arc. Type the measurement then Rise to enter it. To calculate it, enter two other values (pitch, run or diagonal) and press Rise.

Example 7 Inch Pitch 11 Feet 6 Inch Run Rise → RISE 6 ft 8 1/2 in

Run

The horizontal side of a right triangle. It is half the span of a roof (from the outside wall to the centre of the ridge), the length of a stair along the floor, or the chord of an arc. Type the measurement then Run, or calculate it from two other values.

Example 6 Inch Pitch 4 Feet Diag Run → RUN 3 ft 6 15/16 in

Diag

The diagonal (hypotenuse). It is the length of the common rafter, or the diagonal used to check that a foundation is square. Press again for the plumb cut and the level cut.

Example 7 Inch Pitch 7 Feet 2 Inch Run Diag, then Diag twice → DIAGONAL 8 ft 3 9/16 in · PLUMB CUT 30.26° · LEVEL CUT 59.74°

The length is measured point to point: add the roof overhang and subtract half the thickness of the ridge board as needed.

Hip Valley

The length of the hip or valley rafter and its cuts. Enter the pitch and the run of the common rafter, then press Hip Valley. Press again for the plumb cut, the level cut and the cheek cut (bevel). For a roof whose two sides have different pitches, enter the irregular pitch first.

Example 8 Feet 5 Inch Run 7 Inch Pitch Hip Valley, then Hip Valley repeatedly → HIP/VALLEY 12 ft 10 1/2 in · 22.42° · 67.58° · 45.00°

Lengths are theoretical, centreline to centreline.

Compound Mitre

The mitre saw settings for a crown moulding cut flat. Type the corner angle (90 for a square corner), then Compound Mitre: you get the mitre angle (table rotation) and the bevel angle (blade tilt). For a piece with several equal sides, type the number of sides instead (any value under 25 is taken as a number of sides). First check that the stored crown angle matches your moulding.

Example 90 Compound Mitre, then Compound Mitre → MITRE 35.26° · BEVEL 30.00°

Stair

The complete layout of a straight stair. Enter the height to climb with Rise, the length available with Run, or both, then press Stair repeatedly: riser height and number of risers, tread depth and number of treads, rounding differences, stairwell opening length, stringer length, incline. The target riser height and tread depth, the headroom and the floor thickness are set in Preferences. A warning symbol flags a riser higher than the target value.

Example 9 Feet 11 Inch Rise, then Stair repeatedly → RISER HEIGHT 7 7/16 in · 16 risers · TREAD DEPTH 10 in · 15 treads · STAIRWELL OPENING 10 ft 1 in · STRINGER 15 ft 6 15/16 in · 36.64°

The tread depth does not include the nosing. Always check the requirements of the building code.

Arc

Everything about a circular arc. Two values are enough: the diameter and the angle, the diameter and the arc length, or the chord (Run key) and the arc rise (Rise key). Press Arc repeatedly: angle, arc length, chord, segment area, sector area, arc rise, then the length of each stud of an arched wall at the stored on-centre spacing.

Example 5 Feet Circle 85 Arc, then Arc → ARC 85.00° · ARC 3 ft 8 1/2 in

Circle

Area and circumference of a circle. Type the diameter, then Circle; press again for the area, then the circumference.

Example 11 Inch Circle, then Circle twice → AREA 95.03318 in² · CIRCUMFERENCE 34 9/16 in

Jack

The length of each jack rafter. Jack rafters are the shortened rafters that land on the hip or the valley. Enter the pitch and the run, then press Jack repeatedly: on-centre spacing, increment (the difference in length from one jack to the next), each length, then the cuts. The order (descending or ascending) is chosen in Preferences.

Example 8 Feet 5 Inch Run 7 Inch Pitch, then Jack repeatedly → ON CENTRE 16 in · INCREMENT 1 ft 6 1/2 in · 8 ft 2 3/8 in · 6 ft 7 7/8 in · 5 ft 1 3/8 in…

m

Metres. Type a number then m. Two presses give square metres, three give cubic metres. With a value already displayed, m converts it to metres.

Example 8 Feet Conv m → 2.438 m

Length

Stores a length for area, volume and material calculations (blocks, footing, drywall, roofing).

Example 20 Feet 6 Inch Length → LENGTH 20 ft 6 in

Width

Stores the width, then gives, with each press: the area, the square-up diagonal and the perimeter of the rectangle.

Example 20 Feet 6 Inch Length 25 Feet 6 Inch Width, then Width repeatedly → AREA 522.75 ft² · SQUARE-UP 32 ft 8 5/8 in · PERIMETER 92 ft 0 in

Height

Stores the height, then gives, with each press: the volume, the area of the four walls and the room area (walls and ceiling), handy for paint and drywall.

Example 15 Feet Length 20 Feet Width 12 Feet Height, then Height repeatedly → VOLUME 3,600 ft³ · WALLS 840 ft² · ROOM 1,140 ft²

%

Percentages. + 10 % adds 10% to the value, − 20 % takes 20% off, × 18 % calculates 18% of it, ÷ 80 % divides it by 0.80. It also works with measurements. Finally, it is used to enter a pitch as a percentage: 2 % Pitch.

Example 137 Feet Feet + 10 % → 150.7 ft²

Yards, Feet, Inch

The imperial units. Type the number, then the unit: 5 Feet 7 Inch. Two presses of the same unit give square units, three give cubic units. With a value displayed, the key converts; Feet toggles between feet-inches and decimal feet, Inch between fraction and decimals.

Example 5 Feet 7 Inch 1 / 2 + 18 Feet 8 Inch = → 24 ft 3 1/2 in

/

The fraction bar. 1 / 2 gives half an inch. If you do not type the denominator, the current resolution is used: 15 / means 15/16.

Example 15 / = → 15/16 in

Clear

One press clears the display and any error. Two presses also clear the temporary values (rise, run, length, diameter…). The entered pitch, the memories and the stored values are kept.

Example 5 + 3 Clear 4 = → 9

Conv

The conversion and second-function key. Followed by a key, it runs the function written in small type above that key. Followed by a unit, it converts the displayed value. You can also click the small label directly.

Example 14 Feet Conv Yards → 4.666667 yd

Store

Stores a value in memory. Store 1, 2 or 3: three free registers. Store 4 to 9 and 0: the calculator's stored values (block, on-centre spacing, footing section, target riser, floor thickness, target tread, density).

Example 18 Inch Store 5 → ON CENTRE 18 in MEM

Recall

Recalls a value. Followed by a key, it shows what that key holds in memory: Recall 5 shows the on-centre spacing, Recall Run the last run. The recalled value can be used in a calculation.

Example Recall 7 → TARGET RISER 7 1/2 in MEM

M+

The cumulative memory. Each press adds the displayed value to the total. Recall M+ shows the total; M+ after that shows the average, then the number of values. Recall Recall shows the total and clears the memory.

Example 100 M+ 200 M+ 50 Conv M+ Recall M+, then M+ twice → M TOTAL 250 · AVERAGE 83.33333 · COUNT 3

=

Completes the calculation. After a function key, = makes the result available to another function.

Example 2 + 3 × 4 = → 20

⌫

Backspace: undoes the last keystroke of the current entry.

Example 12 3 ⌫ ⌫ 7 Inch = → 17 in

Digits and decimal point

Entering numbers. Type the digits, then the unit if there is one. The point separates the decimals. A number has at most 12 digits. Once a result is displayed, the first digit you type starts a new value.

Example 17.32 Feet = → 17.32 ft

+ − × ÷

The four operations. Calculations are done in the order you key them, with no operator precedence: 2 + 3 × 4 = gives 20. Units follow along: a length times a length gives an area; an area times a length, a volume.

Example 5 Feet 3 Inch × 11 Feet 6 Inch 1 / 2 = → 60.59375 ft²

The second functions, with Conv

Ratio Conv Pitch

Enters the pitch as a ratio of rise ÷ run: 0.65 Conv Pitch.

Example 0.65 Conv Pitch, then Pitch → PITCH RATIO 0.65 · PITCH 7 13/16 in

Rake wall Conv Rise

The length of each stud in a wall whose top follows the roof pitch (gable wall, rake wall). Enter the rise and the run (or the pitch and one of the two), then Conv Rise and Rise repeatedly: on-centre spacing, each stud, base, wall angle. If the rake wall sits on a wall of constant height, type that height just before Conv Rise: it is added to each stud.

Example 4 Feet Rise 8 Feet Run Conv Rise, then Rise repeatedly → ON CENTRE 16 in · 3 ft 4 in · 2 ft 8 in · 2 ft 0 in · 1 ft 4 in · 0 ft 8 in · WALL ANGLE 26.57°

Polygon Conv Run

A regular shape with several sides (hexagonal patio, octagonal gazebo). Enter the radius (15 Feet Conv Arc), type the number of sides, then Conv Run: interior angle, half angle (saw setting), length of one side, perimeter, area.

Example 15 Feet Conv Arc 6 Conv Run, then Run repeatedly → INTERIOR ANGLE 120.00° · HALF ANGLE 60.00° · SIDE 15 ft 0 in · PERIMETER 90 ft 0 in · AREA 584.5671 ft²

Roof Conv Diag
